## Concept

The initial idea was to focus on the nearby Godeok Bridge and connect the school with the bridge's symbolic meaning. During development, I decided that the film should focus more clearly on the school itself. The final concept became **“from a seed to a tree”**: a small beginning grows through learning and experience into a strong student.

The story follows three stages:

1. **Seed** - the beginning of learning.
2. **Growth** - learning with others through classes and experiences.
3. **Strong growth** - developing both knowledge and physical confidence.

## Production Process

I began by researching the school and collecting source information from public school and district materials. I then drafted a storyboard and divided the film into short scenes.

- Planned the message and storyboard.
- Created and refined still images with Nano Banana.
- Wrote prompts and generated short video scenes with Veo 3.
- Reviewed errors in people, movement, and continuity, then regenerated scenes.
- Combined five- and eight-second clips into a film longer than one minute.
- Edited timing, sequence, and transitions with CapCut.

Because Veo 3 responded more consistently to English instructions, prompt writing also became an exercise in learning how to describe camera movement, atmosphere, people, and action precisely in English.

## What Was Difficult

Generating a complete film was not a one-click process. Individual scenes sometimes contained unnatural people or movement, visual details changed between clips, and the generated material did not always follow the storyboard. I repeatedly revised prompts, generated alternatives, selected usable moments, and connected them through editing.

The most important lesson was that AI tools still require a person to define the message, judge the results, recognize errors, and decide what belongs in the final story.

## Result and Reflection

The entry passed the first review but was not selected as a final competition winner. It received an Encouragement Award. The outcome was modest, but the project gave me direct experience with a complete generative AI workflow: research, concept development, visual generation, video generation, prompt iteration, editing, and publication.

For a future version, I would work with classmates, plan character and visual consistency before generation, keep a structured prompt log, and collect feedback from students and teachers before the final edit.
